## Naming and Trademarks
Naming starts with the brand strategy. Whatever is being named, always start with "SUSE" and be as descriptive as possible, following industry standards. All new product and solution names must go through the SUSE Brand Team.

SUSE is a "branded house": one brand (SUSE) for all products, solutions, programs and communities. SUSE follows a unified naming structure and relies on context to simplify its story.

### The SUSE name
* The name began as the German acronym "Software und System-Entwicklung" ("software and system development").
* Today, always write SUSE, never as an acronym.
* SUSE is always in all capital letters with no periods.

### Talking about technology
* Always start at the highest level, SUSE, then speak to the four domains of focus: Linux, cloud native, edge and AI.
* Mention product names only in detailed conversations where context is already set.

### Name patterns
* **Products:** Begin with "SUSE®" followed by a description and context where applicable. Show the registered trademark symbol (®) only on the first mention of SUSE.
* **Programs:** Begin with "SUSE" followed by the name. End with the word "program."
* **Teams:** Begin with "SUSE" followed by the team name and a descriptor where applicable. End with the word "team."
* **Employee networks:** Begin with "SUSE" followed by the network name. End with the words "employee network."

* ❌ Do not include a "v" in versions in public-facing documents.
* ❌ Do not use a capitalized "V" or spaces in versions in technical contexts.

## Icons
The infinity lines are an evolving design element that extends all the way down to icons. Icons make an immediate impact while letting text or color take center stage. Use icons when you need a simple, recognizable representation of an idea.

Illustrations are visual centerpieces for larger designs. Smaller spaces should use SUSE icons.

Icon library access requires credentials from the SUSE brand portal.

Icons are line drawings in two versions:
* Dark version: Pine linework, for White, Fog, Jungle and light shade backgrounds.
* Light version: Fog linework, for Pine backgrounds.

To switch an icon between versions, swap Pine for Fog, or Fog for Pine.

Each icon has one accent color for its accent strokes. Only these combinations are allowed:
* Dark version: Pine with Jungle, or Pine with Waterhole. On Jungle backgrounds, use Pine with Waterhole or Pine with Fog.
* Light version: Fog with Jungle, or Fog with Persimmon.

Use only primary colors for icons. Do not use shades or the infographics palette.

Design principles:
* Strokes have square edges and endings.
* Parts of the icons can be symmetrical, but the overall composition should have asymmetry for balance.
* To further transport the infinity identity, incorporate parallel lines where possible.
* Complex shapes are not closed. If shapes overlap, create a gap between these two elements.

**❌ Icon Don'ts:**
* Don't fill in parts of the icon.
* Don't use more than two colors.
* Don't use hard edges when a curve would be appropriate.
* Don't make icons three dimensional.

## Typography
**Critical:** SUSE has two primary typefaces. Always use one of them, except for the fallback fonts below:
* SUSE, for all headlines and text: https://fonts.google.com/specimen/SUSE?query=suse&preview.script=Latn
* SUSE Mono, for code, data, technical labels and page numbers: https://fonts.google.com/specimen/SUSE+Mono

SUSE does not support Chinese, Japanese, or Korean. If the copy is in Chinese, Japanese, or Korean, use the Noto Sans equivalent.

### The SUSE type family
The SUSE type family is a collection of custom, open source typefaces. It celebrates SUSE's open source roots and its future in the community. SUSE has two typefaces: SUSE and SUSE Mono.

* **SUSE:** a sans serif typeface that is a hybrid between geometric and monospaced features. It defines the brand's voice and personality. It was designed to embody the company's innovative spirit and commitment to open source values. It supports 461 languages and emphasizes clarity and legibility, which suits digital and print applications.
* **SUSE Mono:** a fixed-width typeface that complements the modern, technical style of SUSE. It adds precision and technical credibility. It is especially effective for programming and for presenting data or content where alignment and clarity matter most.
* **Italics:** both families have matching italics. They add nuance and flexibility, introduce subtle emphasis and expressive hierarchy, and let you emphasize technical details while staying consistent in style.

### Fallback fonts
Use a fallback only if absolutely necessary, when SUSE or SUSE Mono is not available.
* **Fallback for SUSE:** Verdana. Apply the same typography rules as the SUSE font.
* **Fallback for SUSE Mono:** Roboto Mono.
* **Chinese, Japanese and Korean copy:** Noto Sans equivalent (SUSE does not support CJK).
* Outside of these cases, never use a font that is not SUSE or SUSE Mono.

### Usage Guidelines
| Element | Font style and weight | Leading | 
| :--- | :--- | :--- | 
| **Headlines:** | SUSE Medium | 110% |
| **Sub Headlines:** | SUSE Semi Bold | 140% |
| **Body:** | SUSE Regular | 140% |
| **Pull quotes:** | SUSE Light | 140% |

* Sub headlines are set at twice the point size of body copy.
* Pull quotes and callout copy are set at 150% of the body copy size.
* Within body copy, use SUSE Bold to draw the eye to key information.
* Use SUSE Italic to highlight key words or phrases and add subtle hierarchy and nuance.
* Always left-align text. Do not use centered or right-aligned formatting.
* Do not set headlines in any weight other than SUSE Medium.

**Important:** NEVER USE ALL CAPS. Use sentence case by default; the only exception is title case for chart and column headings, and headlines as defined below. Exception: SUSE is always written in all capital letters, as are other trademarked names officially written in all caps (for example, SAP and IBM Z).

### Capitalization

Capitalization depends on the kind of text, so identify the element first, then apply the matching rule.

**Headings** are the words at the top of a chart or of spreadsheet columns.

* Capitalization: Write in "Title Case," meaning the first letter in every word is capitalized except for articles (a, an, the), prepositions of three or fewer letters (for, of, on, up, etc.), and conjunctions of three or fewer letters (and, but, nor, or, so, yet, etc.).
* ✅ Examples of written headings: Earnings, Acquisitions & Mergers, Revenue Estimate

**Headlines** are used in blog articles, advertisements, press releases, social media graphics and email subject lines to attract attention and encourage immediate reading.

* Capitalization: Write in "Sentence case," meaning only the first letter of the first word and proper nouns are capitalized.
* Exception: When space is limited in an advertisement and words are broken into 3 or more lines, title case is acceptable.
* ✅ Example of a written headline: SUSE brings the infinite potential of open source to the enterprise.

**Titles** are used to label and describe documents and academic or literary content.

* Capitalization: Write in "Sentence case," meaning only the first letter of the first word and proper nouns are capitalized.
* ✅ Examples of titles: SUSE's writing guide and "10 reasons why you should choose SUSE"

### Type Consistency

Every text element of the same type must use the same font size and the same font weight everywhere it appears. Decide the value once per element — headline, subhead, title, body, caption, label — and apply it identically across every slide, page, and screen in the same piece of work. A title set at 29 px SUSE Medium stays 29 px SUSE Medium on every other slide; it must not become 32 px SUSE Semi Bold elsewhere.

Where a genuine hierarchy is needed, express it by moving to a different, defined element in the type scale rather than by nudging the size or weight of an existing one. Slight variations read as errors rather than as emphasis, and they break the rhythm of a deck or document.

**❌ Typography Don'ts:**
* Don't change the size or weight of a heading from one slide to the next.
* Don't invent intermediate sizes or weights that are not part of the defined type scale.
* Don't resize text to make it fit a layout — adjust the layout or shorten the copy instead.
* Don't mix weights within a single level of hierarchy (for example, some subheads Semi Bold and others Medium).
* Don't leave orphan or widow text (see below).

### Orphans and Widows

Never leave a single word, or a short fragment of a word, stranded on its own line at the end of a paragraph, headline, or subhead — and never let the last line of a paragraph carry over alone to the top of a new column, page, or slide. Stranded text breaks the shape of the text block and draws the eye to the wrong place.

Fix it by rewriting or tightening the copy first, since shorter, sharper text is the better outcome. If the wording has to stand, adjust the text box width or insert a manual line break so the final line carries at least two or three words. Do not fix it by changing the font size, weight, or leading, as that breaks the type consistency rule above.

The same applies to headlines that break awkwardly: break lines at natural phrase boundaries so each line reads as a complete thought, rather than letting the text box decide where the line ends.

## Motion
Movement plays a key role in telling the brand story. SUSE's motion strategy is to move boldly, reflect forward-thinking innovation and bring the infinity stream to life. The direction of animation is always horizontal, representing forward momentum.

### Key principles
* **Easing:** make movements and transitions look natural and less jarring. Always use an ease on animated elements.
* **Staging:** bring focus to what matters most in a scene. Every movement should direct the eye to where it needs to be.
* **Overshooting:** keep a natural cadence. Animations go a little beyond their final pose, then settle back into it. Overshooting applies to graphics, not text.

### Values
* **Simple:** less is more. Emphasize simplicity and clarity so the message is clear. Do not use decorative or flashy motion.
* **Purposeful:** give every motion a clear purpose that guides viewers through the story. Bring meaning and rhythm through anticipation, continuity, pause and action. Do not add unnecessary or ambiguous motion.

### Typography animation
* Follow the typography guidelines.
* Animation should not subtract from legibility.
* Keep all other animation to a minimum.
* Subtly overlapping text animation creates smoother results.
* Give viewers time to read text before moving on, unless the text emphasizes an existing voiceover.
* Text animation examples: push by word or by line, in one or two lines.

### Motion Don'ts
* ❌ Do not curve text.
* ❌ Do not use a strobe or flicker effect.
* ❌ Do not use neon effects.
* ❌ Do not use bouncy, elastic motion on text.

## Merchandise
Each piece of merchandise is a walking advertisement for the brand and often a person's first exposure to SUSE. All SUSE merchandise must be approved by the SUSE Brand Team.

Merchandise rules apply to physical merchandise and can differ from the digital rules elsewhere in this document. For example, black is allowed as a base and logo color on merchandise.

### Material and quality
* Merchandise must uphold SUSE's standards for quality, reliability and performance.
* Always get physical proofs and concepts for large orders to check quality and correct logo usage.
* Materials must be eco-friendly when possible. Ask vendors about their practices, certifications and materials.

### Print options
* **Embroidered:** preferred for high-quality items. Gives a professional look to a luxury item.
* **Spot color:** the most common technique. Use for general printing.
* **Halftone:** for images with different shades, such as gradients.
* **CMYK:** use when printing photos or multicolored graphics on white garments.

### Base colors
Use these canvas colors so the logo and designs stand out: Black, White, Gray and Pine. Gray is a broad term. Suppliers offer different grays, so the exact shade can vary.

### Logo merchandise
* **Size:** allow ample space. The logo should not be too small to read or too large. Do not print it larger than 4.5 inches (11.43 cm).
* **Color:** only the black, white and Jungle green logo colors are allowed. When etching on glass, always use the negative white logo.
* **Placement:** chest area, front panel, side of drinkware and left sleeve only.
* ❌ Do not make the logo too small.
* ❌ Do not repeat the logo.
* ❌ Do not make the logo too large.

### Graphic merchandise
Illustrations and graphic elements can add flavor. Keep the integrity of the original designs and do not overwhelm the canvas.
* ✅ Use a single graphic per design.
* ✅ Use full-color printed graphics.
* ❌ Do not overwhelm a design with graphics.
* ❌ Do not flatten graphics to one or two colors.

## Sound and FX
Sound underpins the expression of SUSE's experiences. Music selection can make or break a video. Always prioritize accessibility when choosing music. For detailed sonic guidelines, contact the SUSE Brand Team.
* **Customer testimonials:** light, upbeat, energetic music that does not overpower the speaker's voice.
* **Promotional videos:** strong voiceovers with an inspiring tone.
* **Commercial videos:** custom music and well-timed sound effects for an immersive experience.
* **Motion graphics videos:** short and to the point, with a clear voiceover and subtle sound effects.
